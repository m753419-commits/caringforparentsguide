Practical help for adult children caring for an aging parent

Family Caregiver Practical Guide publishes plain-language guides for families in the United States. Use them to get organized, talk with your parent and your siblings, and prepare questions for a doctor, an elder law attorney, or a financial professional.

Each guide is general information. It is not medical, legal, or financial advice. The disclaimer explains those limits, and every guide says so again before the details.

How to use a guide

  1. Choose the guide that matches the decision in front of you. You do not need to read them in order.
  2. Copy the checklist or table into a notebook or a shared document. The goal is to see what is missing, not to finish it in one sitting.
  3. Use it as a conversation with your parent, if they are able to take part. Confirm anything legal, medical, or financial with a qualified professional in your parent's state.

Guides

  1. Paperwork & Legal

    What Documents Do You Need When Caring for an Aging Parent? (Checklist)

    If you've ever stood in a hospital hallway trying to remember whether your mom ever signed a health care proxy, you know the feeling. Paperwork isn't the most important part of caring for a parent. Your relationship is. But missing paperwork can turn a hard week into an impossible one.

  2. Sharing Care with Siblings

    How to Split Caregiving Responsibilities Fairly Among Siblings

    In many families, caregiving isn't divided. It lands. It lands on whoever lives closest, whoever has the most flexible job, whoever is the daughter, or whoever said yes first and never got the chance to say "wait." Over time, that person gets tired and resentful, and the other siblings feel shut out or judged.

  3. Finding Care

    In-Home Care vs. Assisted Living: How to Decide What's Right

    "Should Mom stay home with help, or move somewhere with more support?" It's one of the hardest questions families face. There's rarely one right answer, and the right answer can change over time.

  4. Money & Costs

    How Much Does It Cost to Care for an Aging Parent?

    When families first ask "how much will this cost?", they're usually hoping for one number. The honest answer is that it depends: on where your parent lives, how much help they need, what kind of care they choose, and what benefits they qualify for.

  5. Caregiver Self-Care

    Caregiver Burnout: Warning Signs and What to Do About It

    Caring for a parent can be meaningful. It can also be exhausting in a way that sneaks up on you. One week you're managing fine. A few months later you're snapping at your kids, lying awake at 3 a.m., and can't remember the last time you did something just for yourself.

  6. Talking With Your Parent

    How to Talk to Your Aging Parent About Needing Help

    The first time I tried to talk with my mother about getting some help at home, I did everything wrong. I brought it up in the car on the way back from the grocery store, I opened with "Mom, we need to talk about you living alone," and I had a list of things I had already decided. She went quiet, then said she was perfectly fine, and we didn't speak about it again for months.
