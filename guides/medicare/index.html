Medicare & Benefits

Medicare guides for a parent's care

This page helps you find the right Medicare guide. Medicare is U.S. government health insurance for older people and some younger people with disabilities. Start with the one question you have today.

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 7, 2026

US programs only.

This is a lot. You're not doing it wrong. Pick one guide below.

You do not need to read them all.

Please note: This post is general information, not legal, financial, or medical advice.

This is general information, not advice for one person's plan. Check Medicare.gov or call 1-800-MEDICARE before you act. 1-800-MEDICARE is Medicare's official phone line, 1-800-633-4227.

You can also ask Social Security (the federal agency that pays retirement and disability benefits). Or ask SHIP (the State Health Insurance Assistance Program) for free, fair Medicare help (full disclaimer).

When can your parent sign up or change a plan?

Use this part when the question is about timing. Open Enrollment is the yearly time when your parent can change Medicare plans. The other guide is about a move into your home.

    This guide answers what may happen to the plan if your parent moves in.

What should you check about a late sign-up, high bills, and unpaid care?

A penalty is extra money added after a late sign-up. It can last a long time.

Part D is a Medicare drug plan (private insurance approved by Medicare that helps pay for prescription drugs). For a Part D review, ask the plan or the Medicare contractor not connected with the plan. That contractor is an outside group, not the plan.

Part B is Medicare's medical insurance. It helps pay for doctor visits, outpatient care, medical equipment and many preventive services. For Part B, ask Social Security or SHIP.

Long-term daily help means help with bathing, dressing, and using the bathroom.

What should you ask before a hospital stay ends?

Discharge means your parent leaves the hospital. Ask questions before that day.

Home health care is nursing or therapy given at home. It is skilled care (care that needs a nurse or therapist). A doctor must see your parent and order the care.

A fast appeal is a quick review by an outside reviewer.

Hospice is comfort care for someone who is terminally ill. Terminally ill means the illness is expected to lead to the end of life. Respite care is a short break while someone else stays with your parent.

What does Medicare pay for at home?

These guides say what Medicare pays for at home, and what it does not. A medical alert button calls for help when pressed. GPS is a tool that shows where a person is.

Assisted living is housing with help for daily needs. A nursing home is a place where a person can live and get daily care. Dementia is a loss of memory and thinking that gets in the way of daily life.

Memory care is care just for people with dementia. Medicaid is a joint federal and state program that helps with health costs for people with limited income and resources.

Who can talk with Medicare for your parent?

Your parent must give written permission first. Medicare can then talk with you about health facts. That does not let you change the plan alone.

A power of attorney is a document your parent signs to name someone to act for them. Guardianship is when a court gives a person the legal right to decide for someone who can't decide for themselves.

How do you check a notice or appeal a plan decision?

A Medicare Summary Notice (a claims notice, not a bill) is for Original Medicare (regular Medicare).

A Medicare Advantage plan (a Medicare-approved plan from a private company) is different.

One guide covers a plan denial. The other covers care that is already covered and is ending.
