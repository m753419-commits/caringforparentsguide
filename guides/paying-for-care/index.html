Money & Costs

Guides on paying for a parent's care

Most money questions start with one bill or one decision. These guides cover what care costs, how Medicaid may help with a nursing home, getting paid as a family caregiver, tax rules, other help with bills, and comparing nursing homes. Medicaid is a joint federal and state program that helps with health costs for people with limited income and resources.

This is general information, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Rules differ by state. Before you move money, ask your state Medicaid office or an elder law attorney.

An elder law attorney is a lawyer who helps older adults and their families. That help covers state laws, how wishes are carried out, and money options (full disclaimer).

Can you get paid to care for a parent?

Pay can come from a program or from your parent's own money. It can also come from long-term care insurance. That is private insurance that pays for ongoing daily care.
