Missed Medigap Open Enrollment: What to Check Next

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 8, 2026

US programs only.

This is general information, not advice for one person's plan. Check Medicare.gov or call 1-800-MEDICARE before you act. 1-800-MEDICARE is Medicare's official phone line, 1-800-633-4227.

You can also ask Social Security (the federal agency that pays retirement and disability benefits). Or ask SHIP (the State Health Insurance Assistance Program) for free, fair Medicare help (full disclaimer).

Fall Medicare open enrollment (the yearly time to change Medicare plans) is a separate yearly window.

In plain words: Medicare is U.S. government health insurance for older people and some younger people with disabilities. This guide is about Medigap (a private policy that helps pay some Original Medicare (regular Medicare) costs) after its one-time signup period. That one-time federal period does not restart every fall. Fall Medicare open enrollment does not guarantee buying Medigap or an exemption from medical underwriting (a review of health facts used for a yes or a price).

Part B (Medicare's medical insurance; it helps pay for doctor visits, outpatient care, medical equipment and many preventive services) has to be in place for that 6-month period.

The 6 months start the first month your parent is 65 or older and has Part B.

A guaranteed issue right is a right, in set cases, to buy the policies that apply.

The company cannot say no, or charge more, because of health.

That right does not promise Plan N.

Do not drop a current plan yet.

Write the dates down.

Use the notice, not a guess.

A Medicare Advantage plan (a Medicare-approved plan from a private company) is not Medigap.

A trial right is a limited chance to return to Original Medicare and seek a Medigap policy.

Part A (Medicare's hospital insurance; it helps pay for hospital stays, skilled nursing facility care, hospice and some home health care) is named in one trial right below.

Medicare SELECT (a Medigap policy that uses a network of hospitals and may limit doctors) is also named below.

Is Medigap open enrollment the same as the fall period?

No, the Medigap period is once, for 6 months. It starts the first month your parent is 65 or older and has Part B. The fall period does not restart it or drop medical underwriting.

  1. Fall Medicare open enrollment is October 15 to December 7. A change made then usually starts on January 1. It can change Original Medicare, a Medicare Advantage plan, or a Medicare drug plan (Medicare Part D, private insurance approved by Medicare that helps pay for prescription drugs). It does not reopen the 6-month Medigap period.
  2. A Medicare Advantage plan is a Medicare-approved plan from a private company. Its own open enrollment is January 1 to March 31. People already in that type of plan can move to another one. Or they can return to Original Medicare and join a drug plan. That window is not a pass to buy Medigap without a health review.
  3. If Part B was delayed, the 6 months may not have started. If Part B has started, the 6 months run even when a job plan is still in place.
  4. ESRD means end-stage renal disease (a serious kidney condition named on Medicare.gov). Under 65, federal law generally does not require a company to sell Medigap for a disability or for ESRD. At 65, check the right that starts then.

What if the 6 months are already over?

A company may still sell a policy. It may also say no, charge more, or offer fewer choices. A guaranteed issue right is a separate path, and it still does not promise Plan N.

  1. Outside the 6 months, the company may use medical underwriting. If your parent does not meet it, the company may deny the policy.
  2. A pre-existing condition is a health problem already diagnosed or treated. In the regular signup rules, the guide allows a wait of up to 6 months for that problem. The wait looks at care in the 6 months before the policy starts.
  3. Creditable coverage is past coverage that can shorten that wait. At least 6 months in a row can shorten or remove the wait. A break of more than 63 days does not keep the stretch continuous.
  4. Original Medicare still covers its own services during a Medigap wait. Your parent may still pay the share the Medigap policy is not paying yet.
  5. When a guaranteed issue right applies, the guide says there is no wait for pre-existing conditions. That is not the same rule as the general wait. Getting sicker does not by itself create this right.

Which situations can we check?

Check the dates on the notices. The whole situation has to match, not the title alone. This list does not name plan letters, because official pages do not all use the same list.

  1. One case is a Medicare Advantage plan that is leaving Medicare. Or it stops care in your parent's area. Or your parent moves out of the plan's area. Or the plan's network has a significant change. You have this right only if your parent returns to Original Medicare, not to another Medicare Advantage plan. You can often apply from 60 days before coverage ends until 63 days after. Also read the plan's own end notice. Medigap cannot start before that plan ends.
  2. Another case is a job plan that pays after Medicare and is ending. That includes retiree coverage, union coverage, or COBRA (continued job coverage named in the Medicare guide). Count 63 days from the latest of three dates. Those dates are the day coverage ends and the notice date. The third date is a claim denial, if that is how you learned coverage ended. With COBRA, your parent can buy Medigap now or wait until COBRA ends. Not every loss of a job plan fits.
  3. One trial right starts at age 65. Your parent joined a Medicare Advantage plan when Part A first began. They change their mind in the first year. The guide also names PACE (a program that combines medical care and long-term care for older people living in the community). The usual window is 60 days before coverage ends until 63 days after. An extra 12 months is only in some cases. Ask the state insurance department. Do not assume Plan N is included.
  4. The other trial right is for a first switch off a Medigap policy. Your parent dropped Medigap to join a Medicare Advantage plan, or a Medicare SELECT policy, for the first time. They have been in it less than a year and want the old policy back. Ask if the same company still sells it. If not, the guide names a limited set of other policies. That set may not include Plan N. The usual window is again 60 days before until 63 days after. An extra 12 months is not automatic.
  5. A company bankruptcy, or a loss that was not your parent's fault, is another case. The guide gives up to 63 days after the Medigap policy ends. Stopping payment yourself is not this case.
  6. Leaving because the company broke the rules, or misled your parent, is another case. The guide gives 63 days after coverage ends. Keep the papers. Disliking the plan is not enough.

One more case is Original Medicare plus a Medicare SELECT policy.

Your parent moves out of that policy's service area.

The window starts 60 days before that Medicare SELECT policy ends.

It also runs up to 63 days after that end date.

Call the Medicare SELECT company about the options.

What did one state example show?

New York is one state this guide checked, not a rule for every state. A company there that sells Medigap must take an application all year. It cannot say no, or charge more, because of health.

  1. That rule is not a free policy, and it is not every policy. A group policy is only for members of that group.
  2. New York may still use a wait of up to 6 months for a pre-existing condition. Creditable coverage can shorten it when breaks are not more than 63 days.
  3. Ask your own state. This guide did not build a state list. Use the state insurance department list linked from the Medicare guide, and use SHIP.

What order applies if a Medicare Advantage plan is ending?

A right to leave the plan is not a yes on Medigap. Do not cancel the current plan until the new start date is in writing. A drug plan is a separate step.

  1. First confirm the window that lets your parent leave the Medicare Advantage plan. Then confirm the Medigap right, the price, and the start date.
  2. Medigap generally cannot be sold while that plan is still in force, unless your parent is switching back. It cannot start before the Medicare Advantage plan ends.
  3. Medigap policies sold after 2005 do not include prescription drugs. Check a separate Medicare drug plan and its start date. Do not treat "add a drug plan" as a way out of every Medicare Advantage plan. This guide did not map every exception.
  4. A free look is a 30-day period after the new Medigap policy arrives. It applies when you already have a Medigap policy and buy a second one. Do not cancel the first until you decide to keep the second. You pay both premiums (the amount paid, usually each month) for the overlap month. This is not permission to keep a Medicare Advantage plan and Medigap together.

What papers should we keep?

Keep letters, notices, emails, and claim denials from the old coverage. You may need copies with the Medigap application. Get the new yes in writing before you cancel.

  1. Write the age, the state, and the Part A and Part B dates. Write the first Medicare eligibility date. Write whether the coverage now is Original Medicare, a Medicare Advantage plan, or Medigap.
  2. Write the notice date and the coverage end date. Write why the coverage is ending. Do not pick the situation from the heading alone.
  3. If the deadline is close, ask SHIP or the state insurance department now. Do not wait for the fall period.
  4. Plan letters are a separate comparison. See our guide on Plan G and Plan N after you know whether a policy can be bought.

An income-based extra Medicare charge is a different question. See our guide on asking Social Security to review that charge.
