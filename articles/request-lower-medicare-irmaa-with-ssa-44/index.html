Request a Lower Medicare IRMAA With SSA-44

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 8, 2026

US programs only.

Please note: This post is general information, not legal, financial, or medical advice.

This is general information, not advice for one person's plan. Check Medicare.gov or call 1-800-MEDICARE before you act. 1-800-MEDICARE is Medicare's official phone line, 1-800-633-4227.

You can also ask Social Security (the federal agency that pays retirement and disability benefits). Or ask SHIP (the State Health Insurance Assistance Program) for free, fair Medicare help (full disclaimer).

In plain words: This guide shows how a retired parent can ask Social Security to review IRMAA (an extra monthly Medicare charge when income is higher). Medicare is U.S. government health insurance for older people and some younger people with disabilities. The request form is SSA-44 (a Social Security form for a listed life change that lowered income). Start with the premium (the amount paid, usually each month) notice.

The extra charge can apply to Part B (Medicare's medical insurance; it helps pay for doctor visits, outpatient care, medical equipment and many preventive services).

It can also apply to a Medicare drug plan (Medicare Part D, private insurance approved by Medicare that helps pay for prescription drugs) if your parent has one.

It is not a charge from a private supplement policy. Filing the form does not by itself remove the charge.

What is this extra charge?

IRMAA is an extra monthly charge when income is higher. It can be on Part B, a drug plan, or both. It is only on the benefit your parent has (as listed on Social Security's premiums page (checked November 2026), Social Security).

  1. Read the premium notice first. Use it to see the year of the charge and the tax year Social Security used (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  2. The income is not this month's paycheck. It is also not a bank balance (as listed on Social Security's premiums page (checked November 2026), Social Security).
  3. Social Security uses modified adjusted gross income (adjusted gross income on the federal tax return, plus tax-exempt interest, which is interest that is not taxed) (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  4. A drug plan still has its own premium. The extra charge is added on top if your parent has that coverage (as listed on Social Security's premiums page (checked November 2026), Social Security).
  5. Do not file SSA-44 if the notice shows no extra charge. Retirement alone is not a reason to file (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).

Can retirement lead to a new decision?

Sometimes, if your parent or their spouse stopped work or cut hours. That change must have lowered the income Social Security counts. Not every drop in income counts (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).

  1. A life-changing event is a change on Social Security's list. The form lists marriage, divorce, annulment (a legal end of a marriage), and a spouse's death. It also lists stopped work, fewer hours, and some property losses. It also lists some pension losses and some employer settlements (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  2. Stopped work or fewer hours can be the parent or the spouse. The income counted must go down because of that event (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  3. A property loss on the form is not a sale your parent chose. It is also not a transfer your parent chose (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  4. A pension loss means an employer pension stopped, ended, or was reorganized. The form calls that a scheduled change (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  5. An employer settlement on the form is tied to bankruptcy or reorganization. Do not treat every employer payment as this event (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).

The event date must be in the tax year you ask Social Security to use, or in an earlier year (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).

Which income year can we ask Social Security to use?

Do not assume the retirement year is the year to use. The notice shows which tax year Social Security used. The form then explains which newer year to ask for (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).

  1. For 2026 premiums, Social Security generally used the 2024 tax year. Sometimes it used 2023 (as listed on Social Security's premiums page (checked November 2026), Social Security).
  2. Use the form's 2026 examples only as examples. If it used 2024, your parent can ask to use 2025 in one case. Income fell in 2025 because of an event in 2025 or earlier (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  3. Use an estimate of 2026 income in two cases. Income did not fall until 2026, or it fell in 2025 and will be lower in 2026 (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  4. Use 2025 if income fell in 2025 and will not be lower in 2026. That is the form's example for a 2026 premium (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  5. There is a separate case when the notice used a year three years back. Your parent may ask to use the year two years before the premium year. If 2026 used 2023, the form says they can ask to use 2024 (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  6. Add a following-year estimate only if that next year will be even lower. In the form's example, a 2026 main year is followed by 2027 (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  7. An estimate is the income expected on that year's tax return, plus tax-exempt interest. Include the filing status you expect. After filing, send a signed copy. If the estimate changes, tell Social Security. Later corrections can include back charges or refunds. This guide states no refund date and no dollar result (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).

What papers should we include?

Send proof of the life change and of the income for the year you ask them to use. Your parent signs the form. Do not send a Social Security number to this website (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).

  1. If that tax return is already filed, send a signed copy. Or send a transcript from the IRS (the U.S. tax agency), which is an IRS printout of the return (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  2. If you send an estimate, send the signed return after filing. Social Security says it will check the estimate with the IRS later (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  3. For stopped work or fewer hours, the form asks for work papers. Those are an original signed statement from the employer, or copies of pay stubs. Or use original papers, or certified copies (copies an official office has confirmed), that show a business transfer (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  4. If the preferred work papers are missing, ask Social Security what else it can accept. Do not assume that no proof is required (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  5. Other events use other papers. The form names a marriage record, a divorce or annulment decree, and a death record. It also names a letter from the pension plan, or a letter about the employer settlement (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  6. An amended tax return is a different update. Social Security needs the amended return and the receipt from the IRS. If the income figure from the IRS is wrong, correct it with the IRS (as listed on Social Security's premiums page (checked November 2026), Social Security).
  7. Married filing separately, and apart all year, is also different. Call Social Security and explain that. Do not use SSA-44 only for that change (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).

Keep a copy of the notice and of what you send. The form also asks for a phone number and the current mailing address (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).

How do we send the request?

Your parent can sign in, or fax or mail the form and proof to a Social Security office. Or your parent can show the papers to an employee. This guide did not sign in and did not file a form (as listed on Social Security's lower-IRMAA page (checked November 2026), Social Security).

  1. Fax or mail the completed form and the proof to a Social Security office. That is what the public page says (as listed on Social Security's lower-IRMAA page (checked November 2026), Social Security).
  2. Do not use the Baltimore address on the form. That address is for comments about how long the form takes. It is not the filing address (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  3. You can show original papers, or certified copies, to a Social Security employee. Ask which office can take them (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44).
  4. 1-800-772-1213 is Social Security's line for this request. It is not 1-800-MEDICARE. The TTY line (a text phone line) is 1-800-325-0778. The pages do not agree on the opening hour, so this guide does not state one (as listed on Form SSA-44 (checked November 2026), Form SSA-44; Social Security).
  5. The online path says Sign in. Reading the form does not require an account (as listed on Social Security's lower-IRMAA page (checked November 2026), Social Security).

Is this the same as an appeal?

No. A request to use newer income after a listed life change is not an appeal. Social Security says you do not need an appeal for that request. This guide found no single 60-day deadline for SSA-44 (as listed on Social Security's premiums page (checked November 2026), Social Security).

  1. An appeal is for when you disagree with the decision already made. The form for that review is SSA-561-U2 (a form to ask Social Security to look again) (as listed on Social Security's premiums page (checked November 2026), Social Security).
  2. Ask within 60 days after you receive the decision. That limit is on the reconsideration page (a page for a second look at a decision). The 60 days are not counted from the retirement date (as listed on Social Security's reconsideration page (checked November 2026), Social Security).
  3. Read the decision letter for the timing. Also read it before you ask about a late request. This guide does not state a processing time or a refund amount (as listed on Social Security's reconsideration page (checked November 2026), Social Security).
  4. Call CMS (the federal agency that runs Medicare) if the drug-plan record is wrong. Do that when Social Security says your parent has a drug plan and they do not. Use 1-800-MEDICARE. Social Security says it gets those coverage facts from CMS (as listed on Social Security's premiums page (checked November 2026), Social Security).

For current-year premium amounts, use the official premiums page. This guide does not copy the bracket table. One top amount does not match across official versions (as listed on Social Security's premiums page (checked November 2026), Social Security).

Medigap (a private policy that helps pay some Original Medicare (regular Medicare) costs) is a different charge. The guides below are about that policy, not this extra charge.
