Medigap Plan G vs. Plan N for a Parent: Questions to Compare

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 8, 2026

US programs only.

Please note: This post is general information, not legal, financial, or medical advice.

This is general information, not advice for one person's plan. Check Medicare.gov or call 1-800-MEDICARE before you act. 1-800-MEDICARE is Medicare's official phone line, 1-800-633-4227.

You can also ask Social Security (the federal agency that pays retirement and disability benefits). Or ask SHIP (the State Health Insurance Assistance Program) for free, fair Medicare help (full disclaimer).

In plain words: This guide compares standard Medigap Plan G and Plan N (private policies that help pay some Original Medicare (regular Medicare) costs). Medicare is U.S. government health insurance for older people and some younger people with disabilities. These plans relate to Part B (Medicare's medical insurance; it helps pay for doctor visits, outpatient care, medical equipment and many preventive services). Neither standard plan pays the Part B deductible (the amount paid each year before coverage starts), and high-deductible Plan G is a different policy.

Fall Medicare open enrollment (the yearly time to change Medicare plans) does not guarantee buying Medigap or an exemption from medical underwriting (a review for a yes or a price).

Part A (Medicare's hospital insurance; it helps pay for hospital stays, skilled nursing facility care, hospice and some home health care) is the other part these plans can help pay.

A covered mark on the chart means that one listed benefit. It does not mean every medical bill or living cost is paid.

What are standard Plan G and Plan N?

They are Medigap plan names, not parts of Medicare called G or N. Each policy covers one person. You generally need Part A and Part B (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

  1. You cannot use Medigap to pay costs in a Medicare Advantage plan (a Medicare-approved plan from a private company). That is a different path (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  2. Massachusetts, Minnesota, and Wisconsin standardize Medigap in a different way. Do not use this national chart as those states' chart (as listed in the March 2026 Medigap guide, Medicare.gov guide).
  3. People new to Medicare on or after January 1, 2020, cannot buy Plan C or Plan F. That date limit is not a rule that Plan G or Plan N is only for people eligible after 2020 (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  4. Under 65, federal law generally does not require a company to sell Medigap. In some states, companies do offer it. At 65, check the right that starts then (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

What do the two standard plans pay?

On Medicare's chart, the two standard plans match on the listed benefits below. Neither pays the Part B deductible. Standard Plan G pays an excess charge (the difference when a provider may legally charge more than the Medicare-approved amount), and Plan N does not (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

  1. Both show coverage for Part A coinsurance (your percentage share of a covered cost). That includes up to 365 extra hospital days after Medicare hospital benefits are used up (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  2. Both show coverage for Part B coinsurance or a copayment (a set dollar amount for a service). Plan N has extra visit charges in some cases. Those charges are explained below (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  3. Both show coverage for the first 3 pints of blood. Both show coverage for Part A hospice coinsurance or a copayment (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  4. Both show coverage for skilled nursing facility coinsurance. That is not payment for all long-term living care. See our guide on the cost of care (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  5. Both show coverage for the Part A deductible. Neither shows coverage for the Part B deductible. The dollar amount is on the current-year costs page. Official pages do not all show the same 2026 figure. This guide does not print that figure (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov costs).
  6. Standard Plan G shows coverage for that excess charge. Plan N does not. This guide does not state a nationwide percent cap (as listed in the March 2026 Medigap guide, Medicare.gov guide; Medicare.gov).
  7. Ask if the doctor accepts assignment (the provider agrees to the Medicare-approved amount). A doctor who participates with Medicare accepts assignment for Medicare patients (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  8. Plan N still has visit charges in some cases. You pay up to $20 for some office visits. You pay up to $50 for an emergency room visit if your parent is not admitted as an inpatient. Do not treat those amounts as every visit (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  9. Foreign travel emergency is 80 percent, up to the plan's limits. This guide did not list every time limit or lifetime limit. Read the policy (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  10. The chart does not show a yearly out-of-pocket limit (a cap on what you pay yourself) for these two plans. Do not assume a cap like some other Medicare plans (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

How is high-deductible Plan G different?

High-deductible Plan G is not standard Plan G. In some states, you pay Medicare-covered costs up to $2,950 in 2026 before that policy pays. Standard Plan G does not use that threshold (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

  1. Those costs are coinsurance, copayments, and deductibles that Medicare already covers. The $2,950 figure is not a monthly premium (the amount paid, usually each month). It is also not a cap on all care (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  2. Standard Plan G still does not pay the Part B deductible. Do not describe it as a plan with nothing to pay (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  3. The March 2026 guide describes a separate deductible for foreign-travel emergency. Do not fold that separate amount into the $2,950 figure. Read the guide for that benefit (as listed in the March 2026 Medigap guide, Medicare.gov guide).
  4. Ask whether high-deductible Plan G is sold where your parent lives. This guide has no company prices and names no company (as listed in the March 2026 Medigap guide, Medicare.gov guide).

What should we ask before comparing price?

Get a written premium for the same letter and the same type, standard or high-deductible. This guide has no prices and does not recommend a policy (as listed in the March 2026 Medigap guide, Medicare.gov guide).

  1. Write the state and the age. Write the Part A and Part B start dates. Write when Medicare eligibility first began. Write whether your parent has Original Medicare or a Medicare Advantage plan (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  2. Ask if the policy is Medicare SELECT (a Medigap policy that uses a network of hospitals and may limit doctors). Compare that limit along with the premium (as listed in the March 2026 Medigap guide, Medicare.gov guide).
  3. Ask whether the company will use medical underwriting. Where you live, discounts, and that review can change the price (as listed in the March 2026 Medigap guide, Medicare.gov guide).
  4. Medigap policies sold after 2005 do not include prescription drugs. Your parent may need a separate Medicare drug plan (Medicare Part D, private insurance approved by Medicare that helps pay for prescription drugs) (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  5. If a Medigap policy is already in place, do not cancel it first. Read our guide on a missed Medigap enrollment before you drop it (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

Does fall enrollment let us buy either plan?

No. Fall Medicare open enrollment does not guarantee buying Medigap. It also does not guarantee an exemption from medical underwriting (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

  1. The one-time Medigap period is 6 months. It is not the fall window. Our missed-enrollment guide lists what to check next (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  2. A right to buy does not mean every letter, including Plan N. Official pages do not all name the same policies. Use the current page for the case you have (as listed in the March 2026 Medigap guide, Medicare.gov guide).
  3. An extra Medicare charge based on income is separate from this premium. See our guide on asking Social Security to review that charge.
