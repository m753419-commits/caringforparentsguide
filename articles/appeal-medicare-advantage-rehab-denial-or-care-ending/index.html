Medicare Advantage Rehab Denial or Care Ending

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 7, 2026

US programs only.

Please note: This post is general information, not legal, financial, or medical advice.

This is general information, not advice for one person's plan. Check Medicare.gov or call 1-800-MEDICARE before you act. 1-800-MEDICARE is Medicare's official phone line, 1-800-633-4227.

You can also ask Social Security (the federal agency that pays retirement and disability benefits). Or ask SHIP (the State Health Insurance Assistance Program) for free, fair Medicare help (full disclaimer).

In plain words: This guide is about care a plan did not approve. It is also about covered care that is ending. The plan is a Medicare Advantage plan (a Medicare-approved plan from a private company). Medicare is U.S. government health insurance for older people and some younger people with disabilities.

A Medicare Cost plan is a type of Medicare health plan available in some areas (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

In a Medicare Cost plan, an appeal can be for services you got outside of the plan's network (the doctors and hospitals a plan works with) (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

In a Medicare Cost plan, an appeal for those services outside the plan's network follows the Original Medicare (regular Medicare) appeals process (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

This guide does not describe any other appeal in a Medicare Cost plan.

See our Medicare Summary Notice guide for that appeal path.

An organization determination is the plan's first decision (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).

It can be about approval or payment, what your parent pays, or a limit on how much care (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).

That first decision is not an appeal (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).

A reconsideration is the first appeal. It asks the plan to look at that decision again (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).

A fast appeal is also called an expedited appeal (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

The BFCC-QIO (Beneficiary and Family Centered Care Quality Improvement Organization) is the outside reviewer when covered care is ending (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

The BFCC-QIO is not the health plan (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

A confusing denial is common. Read the notice name. Next, use only the path that matches it.

How do I tell a plan denial from care that is ending?

A denial of care not yet approved goes to the plan. Covered care that is ending can go to the BFCC-QIO. Read the notice name before you call (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov; CMS).

  1. Start with the plan's organization determination and the denial notice. If you disagree, file a reconsideration with the Medicare Advantage plan (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  2. The plan may use an IDN (Integrated Denial Notice, a notice that care or payment was denied in whole or in part). Its other name is Notice of Denial of Medical Coverage or Payment (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  3. The form is CMS-10003. The download line says Eff Jan 2025. It is not a 2026 form (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  4. An IDN can also be used when the plan stops or cuts care it already approved. Do not treat an IDN as only for care that never started (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).

A SNF is a skilled nursing facility, a place that gives skilled nursing care (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

An IM is the Important Message from Medicare, the hospital notice of the right to appeal a discharge (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).

A NOMNC is a Notice of Medicare Non-Coverage, the notice that Medicare-covered services are ending (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).

The IDN, the IM, and the NOMNC are different notices in these sources.

Do not guess the path from the word rehab.

If the stay is inpatient rehab, confirm the inpatient status and the notice name. This guide did not verify every inpatient rehab exception.

How do I appeal care the plan has not approved?

File a reconsideration with the plan within 65 calendar days from the date of the notice. A standard request is written, unless the plan accepts a spoken one (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov; CMS).

  1. Medicare.gov says you, your representative, or your doctor or other health care provider files within 65 days. CMS (the federal agency that runs Medicare) says the enrollee, the representative, or the physician may appeal. An enrollee is the person in the plan (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov; CMS).
  2. If you miss the date, you must give a reason (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  3. A fast request may be spoken or written (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  4. Call the plan, or check the Evidence of Coverage, to see if a spoken standard request is allowed. An Evidence of Coverage is the plan booklet of what it covers. This guide did not read one plan's booklet (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  5. Include the service, the dates, why you disagree, and any representative proof. A doctor's note can help. Follow the denial notice (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

How long does that plan appeal take?

For care not yet received, the plan has up to 30 calendar days. For a payment appeal after care was given, it has up to 60 calendar days. These clocks are not the first approval time (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).

  1. The plan must decide as quickly as your parent's health requires, and no later than those limits (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  2. This guide did not find a deadline for the first approval request. Do not reuse these appeal times for that first request (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  3. In some cases the plan may take up to 14 more days. For example, it may need facts from a non-contract provider, and the extra time is in your parent's best interest (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov). A non-contract provider is a provider with no contract with the plan.
  4. The plan must send a written notice of an extension. The notice gives the reason and the right to disagree. Not every case can be extended (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  5. These day counts are the current pages. They are not labeled as one 2026 rule book (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov; CMS).

When can I ask the plan for a faster decision?

Ask the plan when a wait may seriously jeopardize life, health, or the ability to regain maximum function. The fast decision is due within 72 hours when that path applies. Not every rehab denial gets a 72-hour decision (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

  1. If the plan finds that risk, it must decide within 72 hours. It must also do that if your doctor tells the plan about the risk (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  2. If a physician requests the expedited reconsideration, the plan must expedite it (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  3. Ask the doctor to explain the risk of waiting. A fast review does not promise the plan will approve the care (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  4. This fast plan appeal goes to the plan. It is not the BFCC-QIO review for care that is already ending (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

What if the hospital is ending the stay?

Call the BFCC-QIO listed on the IM, no later than the day your parent is scheduled to leave. Do not use the NOMNC noon deadline for this hospital step (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

  1. Hospitals give the IM to inpatients in Original Medicare and in a Medicare Advantage plan. The form is CMS-10065. These form numbers are not labeled as a 2026 edition (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  2. Your parent should get the IM within 2 days of admission and before discharge. If you do not get it, ask for it (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  3. If that IM is more than 2 days before the discharge day, a follow-up is required. The hospital must give a copy of the signed IM, or a new IM to sign, before discharge (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  4. A DND is a Detailed Notice of Discharge, the hospital notice that explains the reasons for discharge. The hospital gives it by noon the day after the BFCC-QIO notifies the hospital or the plan. The form is CMS-10066. It is given only if an appeal is requested (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov; CMS).
  5. In the hospital, the BFCC-QIO decides within one day of getting the information it asked for (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

Commence or Acentra runs the BFCC-QIO, depending on the state. Use the one named on your parent's notice (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

What if skilled nursing or other covered care is ending?

Use the NOMNC, and call the BFCC-QIO no later than noon the day before the end date on it. This noon rule is not the hospital rule (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

  1. An HHA is a home health agency, an agency that gives care at home. It is one provider type named when covered services are ending (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov). This guide does not restate home health eligibility here. Our hospital guide covers home health rules.
  2. A CORF is a comprehensive outpatient rehabilitation facility, outpatient rehab that is not every place called rehab (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  3. In plain terms, hospice means comfort care for a person who is terminally ill. Terminally ill means the illness is expected to lead to the end of life. On this notice, hospice is one of the listed provider types (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  4. Home health agencies, SNFs, hospices, and CORFs give a NOMNC when covered services are ending. They should give it at least 2 days before covered services end. If you do not get this notice, ask for it. The download line says Eff Jan 2025. This page's main text did not list form numbers, so this guide does not guess them (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov; CMS).
  5. A DENC is a Detailed Explanation of Non-Coverage, the notice that explains why covered services are ending outside the hospital. After you ask, the provider gives it by the end of that day. It is given only if a fast review is requested. The BFCC-QIO decides by close of business the day after it gets the information it needs. The page does not say this skips weekends or holidays (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov; CMS).

What might my parent still have to pay?

A review does not mean your parent pays nothing. A deductible (the amount paid each year before coverage starts) or coinsurance (your share of the cost) can still apply. Hospital rules and SNF rules are not the same (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

  1. If the hospital appeal meets the deadline, your parent can stay while you wait. Your parent does not have to pay for that stay, except applicable coinsurance or deductibles (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  2. If the review says the hospital stay should end, and the deadline was met, there is a noon limit. Your parent is not responsible through noon of the day after you get the decision, except applicable coinsurance or deductibles. After that noon, your parent may have to pay (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  3. If the plan never authorized the inpatient stay, you may still need another appeal. The same is true when the stay was not emergency or urgently needed care. A BFCC-QIO review does not by itself promise the plan will pay (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  4. Do not copy the hospital payment rule onto a SNF or other non-hospital service. If the review says that coverage is ending too soon, Medicare may keep covering it, depending on care needs. Applicable coinsurance or deductibles can still apply (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  5. If the review says non-hospital coverage should end, your parent is not responsible for services before the NOMNC end date. If services continue after that date, your parent may have to pay (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

If you miss the hospital deadline, you can still ask the BFCC-QIO to review. Different rules apply, and your parent might pay for days past the first discharge date. Your parent can also ask the plan, but different rules apply (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

If you miss the non-hospital BFCC-QIO deadline, you can ask the plan for a fast reconsideration. Services are covered only if the decision is in your favor (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

What if the plan says no again?

The plan must send a full or partial denial to the IRE (Independent Review Entity, a reviewer outside the plan). You do not start that step at the BFCC-QIO (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov; CMS).

  1. The IRE reviews the plan's decision. It is not the BFCC-QIO (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).
  2. Pre-service IRE appeals are 30 days. Payment appeals are 60 days. Fast appeals are 72 hours. A similar extension of up to 14 days can apply in some cases (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  3. You have 60 days from the date of the IRE decision to ask for level 3. Level 3 is OMHA (the Office of Medicare Hearings and Appeals, the office that can hold a hearing). For 2026, the case must be at least $200. That amount is not a fee to start the appeal (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  4. You have 60 days after you get the level 3 decision to ask the Medicare Appeals Council. That council is level 4 (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  5. You have 60 days after you get the Council's decision to ask a federal court. For 2026, the case must be at least $1,960. These 60-day periods are not labeled as a 2026 rule (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

Follow the decision letter for where to send the next request.

A QIC (Qualified Independent Contractor, an outside reviewer) is Original Medicare's level 2 reviewer (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

The QIC did not take part in the level 1 decision (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

The Medicare Advantage level 2 name on these pages is the IRE (as listed on CMS.gov (checked November 2026), CMS).

What should I copy down today?

Copy what the notice already prints, and act on the notice's own date. This worksheet is ours, not a new government form (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).

  1. Copy the notice name, the date, the service end date, and the office that receives it. Copy the phone number too (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  2. Mark whether this is a new-service denial, a payment dispute, or care that is ending (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  3. Ask the doctor for a note on need and on the risk of delay. Gather the service name, dates, denial reason, support papers, and representative proof (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  4. Act on the day the notice requires. Keep the time of calls and of what you sent, plus any confirmation (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
  5. Do not send your parent's name, Medicare number, or health facts to this website. Do not stop treatment on your own from this guide. Confirm the case with the office named on the notice (as listed on Medicare.gov (checked November 2026), Medicare.gov).
