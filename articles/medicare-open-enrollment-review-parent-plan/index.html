How to Help Your Parent Review Medicare Plans During Open Enrollment

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 3, 2026

US programs only.

Please note: This post is general information, not legal, financial, or medical advice.

  1. When is Medicare Open Enrollment, and what can my parent change?
  2. How do I compare plans for my parent on Medicare Plan Finder?
  3. What should we look for in the Annual Notice of Change?
  4. Can I make Medicare plan changes for my parent?
  5. What if we miss the Open Enrollment deadline?

When is Medicare Open Enrollment, and what can my parent change?

[待核 Q1, Q2]: Open Enrollment dates, the date new coverage starts, and the changes allowed go here, quoted from Medicare.gov.

  1. Find the plan's yearly notice first. [待核 Q4]
  2. Write down what changes next year for your parent's costs, drugs, and doctors. [待核 Q4]
  3. Decide whether to keep the plan or compare others on Medicare Plan Finder.
  4. If your parent has a Medigap policy (extra insurance that helps pay Original Medicare costs), read the Medigap question below before changing anything. [待核 Q3]

How do I compare plans for my parent on Medicare Plan Finder?

[待核 Q6]: what Plan Finder compares and whether your parent needs to log in, quoted from Medicare.gov.

  1. Sit down together and gather your parent's Medicare card, a list of every drug with its dose, and the pharmacies they use.
  2. Enter the drugs and pharmacies on Plan Finder. [待核 Q6]
  3. Compare the total yearly cost, not just the monthly premium (the amount paid each month for the plan). [待核 Q6]
  4. Check each doctor by calling the doctor's office and the plan to ask whether the doctor is in the plan's network (the doctors and hospitals the plan works with).
  5. Look at the Star Ratings. [待核 Q7]

What should we look for in the Annual Notice of Change?

[待核 Q4, Q5]: when the Annual Notice of Change (the letter a plan sends each fall listing next year's changes) arrives and what Medicare says to check, quoted from Medicare.gov.

  1. Premium and other costs. [待核 Q4]
  2. Drug list: check every drug your parent takes against the formulary (the list of drugs a plan covers). [待核 Q4]
  3. Doctors, hospitals, and pharmacies: check whether any have left the network. [待核 Q4]
  4. Keep the Evidence of Coverage too. [待核 Q5]

Can I make Medicare plan changes for my parent?

[待核 Q10]: what authorization Medicare needs before it can talk with you, and whether a family member can enroll for a parent.

  1. Ask about the authorization form your parent can sign so Medicare can talk with you. [待核 Q10]
  2. Call together, with your parent on speaker, so they can give permission on the call. [待核 Q12]
  3. Get free help from SHIP (State Health Insurance Assistance Program: free, unbiased Medicare counseling): shiphelp.org or 1-877-839-2675. [待核 Q11]
  4. Watch for scam calls. [待核 Q16]

What if we miss the Open Enrollment deadline?

[待核 Q8, Q9]: what the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period allows and which Special Enrollment Periods may apply, quoted from Medicare.gov.

  1. If your parent is in a Medicare Advantage Plan (a private plan that replaces Original Medicare), check the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period. [待核 Q8]
  2. Check whether a Special Enrollment Period applies (a window to change plans outside the usual times, for example after a move). [待核 Q9] If your parent moved, see what happens to Medicare when a parent moves.
  3. Check the Medigap rules on Medicare.gov before dropping or switching a policy. [待核 Q3]
  4. Ask SHIP (1-877-839-2675) to confirm what your parent can still do.

What will Medicare cost in 2027?

[待核 Q13, Q14]: 2027 amounts go here only if CMS has published them; anything unpublished will say "not yet published."

  1. Part B premium and deductible for 2027. [待核 Q13a]
  2. Part D out-of-pocket limit and deductible for 2027. [待核 Q13b, Q13c]
  3. Average plan premiums for 2027. [待核 Q13d, Q14]
  4. If cost is a worry, look at Extra Help (Medicare's program that helps people with limited income pay drug costs) and Medicare Savings Programs (state help paying Medicare costs). See also Medicare late enrollment penalties. [待核 Q13e]
