How to Help Your Parent Review Medicare Plans During Open Enrollment

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 3, 2026

US programs only.

Please note: This post is general information, not legal, financial, or medical advice.

Before your parent changes plans, confirm the details with Medicare.gov, 1-800-MEDICARE (Medicare's official phone line, 1-800-633-4227), or SHIP (State Health Insurance Assistance Program: free, unbiased Medicare counseling) (full disclaimer).

When is Medicare Open Enrollment, and what can my parent change?

Yes, from October 15 to December 7, 2026: Open Enrollment is the time each year when you can make changes to your coverage, so your parent can switch between Original Medicare (regular Medicare) and a Medicare Advantage Plan (a Medicare-approved plan from a private company), or join, drop, or switch a Medicare Advantage Plan or drug plan, and changes take effect January 1, 2027 if the plan gets the request by December 7 (Medicare.gov).

  1. Find the plan's yearly notice first. Medicare & You 2027 says the plan sends the Annual Notice of Change (the yearly letter from your parent's plan about next year's changes) by September 30 (Medicare & You 2027).
  2. Write down what changes in January in your parent's coverage and costs.
  3. Decide whether to keep the plan or compare others on Medicare Plan Finder (Medicare.gov's tool for comparing plans).
  4. If your parent may switch to Original Medicare, Medicare.gov says they may need to join a separate drug plan and may want to add Medigap (extra insurance from a private company that helps pay your share of costs in Original Medicare), and there are limits on when they can add it (Medicare.gov); see the deadline question below.

How do I compare plans for my parent on Medicare Plan Finder?

Use Plan Finder with or without logging in: CMS (the federal agency that runs Medicare) says it lets people compare Medicare health and drug plans, including premiums (the amount paid for a plan, usually each month), costs, benefits, and other coverage information (CMS, 2026).

  1. Sit down together and gather your parent's Medicare card and a list of every drug they take.
  2. Log in or answer a few questions. Medicare.gov says logging in gives a summary of current coverage; without an account, you answer a few questions first so Plan Finder can give better cost estimates (Plan Finder).
  3. Know the login options: CMS says Medicare accounts can be accessed with Login.gov, ID.me, and CLEAR (CMS, 2026).
  4. Compare premiums, costs, and benefits side by side for each plan your parent is considering (CMS, 2026).
  5. Look at the Star Ratings. Plans are rated from 1 to 5 stars on quality and performance, and the 2027 ratings appeared on Plan Finder on October 8, 2026 (CMS, 2026; Medicare & You 2027).

What should we look for in the Annual Notice of Change?

Look for any changes in coverage, costs, and more that start in January: Medicare & You 2027 says the plan sends this notice by September 30 (Medicare & You 2027).

  1. Read the Annual Notice of Change and review any changes to decide whether the plan will still meet their needs (Medicare.gov).
  2. If it didn't arrive, contact the plan, as Medicare.gov advises (Medicare.gov).
  3. Keep the Evidence of Coverage too. It gives details about what the plan covers and how much your parent pays next year, and Medicare & You 2027 says the plan sends it (or a notice) by October 15 (Medicare & You 2027; Medicare.gov).
  4. Get free help reading them: SHIP counselors can help compare costs, review coverage, and check provider networks (the doctors and hospitals a plan works with) during Open Enrollment (shiphelp.org).

Can I make Medicare plan changes for my parent?

Possibly, with your parent: CMS-10106 only lets Medicare talk to you about claims and health records; to change plans, call 1-800-MEDICARE together or ask SHIP (Medicare.gov).

  1. Have your parent sign the form "Authorization to Disclose Personal Health Information" (CMS-10106) so Medicare can talk with you; get it from CMS.
  2. To change plans, call 1-800-MEDICARE together (1-800-633-4227; TTY, a text phone line for people who are deaf or hard of hearing: 1-877-486-2048), with your parent on the call.
  3. Or ask SHIP together: shiphelp.org or 1-877-839-2675.
  4. Watch for scam calls. Medicare.gov says Medicare will never call to sell you anything, and not to share a Medicare Number with anyone who contacts you unless you contacted them first (Medicare.gov; Medicare.gov).

What if we miss the Open Enrollment deadline?

Possibly: If your parent is already in a Medicare Advantage Plan, they can switch plans or return to Original Medicare once from January 1 to March 31, and certain life events allow a Special Enrollment Period (a window to change plans when certain life events happen) (Medicare.gov; Medicare & You 2027).

  1. Check the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (January 1–March 31): your parent can switch to another Medicare Advantage Plan, or drop it and return to Original Medicare and join a separate drug plan (Medicare.gov).
  2. See whether a Special Enrollment Period applies, such as moving out of the plan's service area (the area where the plan is offered), living in or leaving a nursing home, leaving employer or union coverage, losing Medicaid (a joint federal and state program that helps with health costs for people with limited income and resources), having Medicare and Medicaid or Extra Help (Medicare's program that helps people with limited income pay drug costs), or joining a 5-star plan (Medicare.gov). If your parent moved, see what happens to Medicare when a parent moves.
  3. Check Medigap before switching. Outside the 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period, in most cases there's no federal right to switch Medigap policies, and the insurer can deny a policy based on medical underwriting (reviewing health history to decide whether to sell a policy) (Medicare.gov; Medicare.gov).
  4. Ask about state rights and next steps: check with your State Insurance Department, or call SHIP at 1-877-839-2675 (Medicare.gov).

What will Medicare cost in 2027?

Partly announced: For Part B (doctor and outpatient coverage), 2027 amounts haven't been announced yet; the 2026 Part B premium is $202.90 a month (Medicare.gov).

  1. Part D (drug coverage) limits for 2027: out-of-pocket (what you pay yourself) spending on covered drugs is capped at $2,400 in 2027 ($2,100 in 2026), and no drug plan may have a deductible (the amount paid before the plan starts to pay) over $700 in 2027 ($615 in 2026) (Medicare.gov).
  2. Average premiums for 2027: CMS projects the average monthly Medicare Advantage premium will fall from $14.37 in 2026 to $12.00 in 2027, and the average stand-alone Part D (a drug plan bought separately, used with Original Medicare) premium will rise from $35.09 in 2026 to $36 in 2027 (CMS, September 28, 2026).
  3. Spread drug costs over the year: every Medicare drug plan offers the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan (a payment option that spreads drug costs over the year), which applies in 2026 and later years, costs nothing to join, and starts by contacting the plan (Medicare.gov; CMS).
  4. If cost is a worry, look at Extra Help and Medicare Savings Programs (state help paying Medicare costs); see also Medicare late enrollment penalties.
