How to Help Your Parent Review Medicare Plans During Open Enrollment

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 7, 2026

US programs only.

Before your parent changes plans, check the details. Use Medicare.gov, 1-800-MEDICARE (Medicare's official phone line, 1-800-633-4227), or SHIP. SHIP is the State Health Insurance Assistance Program: free, fair Medicare help (full disclaimer).

In plain words: Medicare is U.S. government health insurance for older people and some younger people with disabilities. Each fall, your parent can change Medicare plans. They don't have to change, but it pays to check. First, find the letter from their plan about next year's changes.

When is Medicare Open Enrollment, and what can my parent change?

Your parent can switch between Original Medicare (regular Medicare) and a Medicare Advantage Plan (a Medicare-approved plan from a private company). Open Enrollment runs from October 15 to December 7, 2026. Changes start January 1, 2027 if the plan gets the request by December 7 (Medicare.gov).

  1. Know what your parent can change. Open Enrollment is the time each year to change coverage. Your parent can switch between Original Medicare and a Medicare Advantage Plan. They can also join, drop, or switch a Medicare Advantage Plan or a Medicare drug plan (Medicare Part D, private insurance approved by Medicare that helps pay for prescription drugs).
  2. Find the plan's yearly notice first. It is called the Annual Notice of Change. It is the yearly letter from your parent's plan about next year's changes. Medicare & You 2027 says the plan sends it by September 30 (Medicare & You 2027).
  3. Write down what changes in January in your parent's coverage and costs.
  4. Decide whether to keep the plan or compare others. Use Medicare Plan Finder, Medicare.gov's tool for comparing plans.
  5. If your parent may switch to Original Medicare, they may need to join a separate drug plan, Medicare.gov says. They may also want to add Medigap. Medigap is extra insurance from a private company. It helps pay your share of costs in Original Medicare. There are limits on when they can add it (Medicare.gov). See the deadline question below.

How do I compare plans for my parent on Medicare Plan Finder?

Use Plan Finder with or without logging in. CMS (the federal agency that runs Medicare) says it compares health and drug plans, including premiums (the amount paid for a plan, usually each month), costs, and benefits (CMS, 2026).

  1. Sit down together and gather your parent's Medicare card and a list of every drug they take.
  2. Log in or answer a few questions. Medicare.gov says logging in shows a summary of current coverage. Without an account, you answer a few questions first. Then Plan Finder can give better cost estimates (Plan Finder).
  3. Know the login options: CMS says you can get into a Medicare account with Login.gov, ID.me, and CLEAR (CMS, 2026).
  4. Compare premiums, costs, and benefits side by side for each plan your parent is thinking about (CMS, 2026).
  5. Look at the Star Ratings. Plans are rated from 1 to 5 stars on quality and how well they work. The 2027 ratings showed up on Plan Finder on October 8, 2026 (CMS, 2026; Medicare & You 2027).

What should we look for in the Annual Notice of Change?

Look for changes in coverage, costs, and more that start in January. Medicare & You 2027 says the plan sends this notice by September 30 (Medicare & You 2027).

  1. Read the Annual Notice of Change and look over any changes. Then decide if the plan will still meet your parent's needs (Medicare.gov).
  2. If it didn't arrive, contact the plan, as Medicare.gov advises (Medicare.gov).
  3. Keep the Evidence of Coverage too. It tells what the plan covers and how much your parent pays next year. Medicare & You 2027 says the plan sends it (or a notice) by October 15 (Medicare & You 2027; Medicare.gov).
  4. Get free help reading them: SHIP counselors can help compare costs and review coverage. They can also check provider networks during Open Enrollment. A network is the doctors and hospitals a plan works with (shiphelp.org).

Can I make Medicare plan changes for my parent?

Possibly, with your parent: Form CMS-10106 only lets Medicare talk to you about claims and health records. To change plans, call 1-800-MEDICARE together or ask SHIP (Medicare.gov).

  1. Have your parent sign the form "Authorization to Disclose Personal Health Information" (CMS-10106). Then Medicare can talk with you. Get it from CMS.
  2. To change plans, call 1-800-MEDICARE together (1-800-633-4227), with your parent on the call. TTY is a text phone line for people who are deaf or hard of hearing. The TTY number is 1-877-486-2048.
  3. Or ask SHIP together: shiphelp.org or 1-877-839-2675.
  4. Watch for scam calls. Medicare.gov says Medicare will never call to sell you anything. Don't share a Medicare Number with anyone who contacts you, unless you contacted them first (Medicare.gov; Medicare.gov).

What if we miss the Open Enrollment deadline?

Possibly. In a Medicare Advantage Plan, your parent can switch or go back to Original Medicare once, January 1–March 31 (Medicare.gov; Medicare & You 2027).

  1. Check the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (January 1–March 31). Your parent can switch to another Medicare Advantage Plan. Or they can drop it, go back to Original Medicare, and join a separate drug plan (Medicare.gov).
  2. See whether a Special Enrollment Period (SEP, a special chance to switch plans because of a life event such as a move) applies. Examples include moving out of the plan's service area (the area where the plan is offered). Others are living in or leaving a nursing home, or leaving job or union coverage. Others are losing Medicaid, or having Medicare and Medicaid or Extra Help. Medicaid is a joint federal and state program. It helps with health costs for people with limited income and resources. Extra Help is Medicare's program that helps people with low income pay drug costs. Joining a 5-star plan is another example (Medicare.gov). If your parent moved, see what happens to Medicare when a parent moves.
  3. Check Medigap before switching. There is a 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period. Outside it, there's most often no federal right to switch Medigap policies. The insurer can say no based on medical underwriting. That means looking at health history to decide whether to sell a policy (Medicare.gov; Medicare.gov).
  4. Ask about state rights and next steps. Check with your State Insurance Department, or call SHIP at 1-877-839-2675 (Medicare.gov).

What will Medicare cost in 2027?

Part B (Medicare's medical insurance; it helps pay for doctor visits, outpatient care, medical equipment and many preventive services) amounts for 2027 aren't out yet. The 2026 Part B premium is $202.90 a month (Medicare.gov).

  1. Part D (drug coverage) limits for 2027: Out-of-pocket costs are what you pay yourself. For covered drugs, they are capped at $2,400 in 2027 ($2,100 in 2026). No drug plan may have a deductible over $700 in 2027 ($615 in 2026). A deductible is the amount paid before the plan starts to pay (Medicare.gov).
  2. Average premiums for 2027: These are CMS projections. The average monthly Medicare Advantage premium is projected to go from $14.37 in 2026 to $12.00 in 2027. A stand-alone Part D plan is a drug plan bought on its own, used with Original Medicare. Its average premium is projected to go from $35.09 in 2026 to $36 in 2027 (CMS, September 28, 2026).
  3. Spread drug costs over the year: Every Medicare drug plan offers the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan. It is a payment option that spreads drug costs over the year. It applies in 2026 and later years. It costs nothing to join. To start, contact the plan (Medicare.gov; CMS).
  4. If cost is a worry, look at Extra Help and Medicare Savings Programs. These are state programs that help pay Medicare costs. See also Medicare late enrollment penalties.
