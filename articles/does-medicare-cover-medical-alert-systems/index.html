Does Medicare Cover Medical Alert Systems?

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 7, 2026.

US programs only.

Medicare is U.S. government health insurance for older people and some younger people with disabilities. Original Medicare is regular Medicare. A Medicare Advantage plan is a Medicare-approved plan from a private company. Medicaid is a joint federal and state program that helps with health costs for people with limited income and resources.

In plain words: This guide is about whether Medicare pays for a medical alert button. Original Medicare does not pay for these systems. A Medicare Advantage plan or Medicaid might. First, check which coverage your parent has.

Questions in this guide

  2. Do Medicare Advantage plans cover medical alert systems?

Does Medicare cover life alert systems? If not, why?

No, Original Medicare doesn't cover them. CMS (the federal agency that runs Medicare) says in its national equipment list: "Deny - these are emergency communications systems and do not serve a diagnostic or therapeutic purpose" (CMS NCD 280.1).

Part B covers durable medical equipment (DME). That is equipment such as walkers and hospital beds. It is primarily for a medical purpose. A medical alert button isn't counted as DME. A doctor's prescription doesn't change the answer.

  1. Don't count on Medigap. Medicare Supplement Insurance (Medigap) helps pay your parent's share of costs (Medicare.gov). Those costs are in Original Medicare. Original Medicare doesn't cover alert systems. So there is generally no share for Medigap to pay.
  2. If your parent has Medicare Advantage, check the plan (next question).
  3. If your parent has Medicaid or limited income, check state programs (third question).
  4. If no program pays, compare systems on these points. In-home use versus mobile (cellular/GPS) use. Who answers the call? A 24/7 monitoring center, or family only. Fall detection and its fee. Check cellular coverage. Check it where your parent lives. Water resistance for the shower. Check battery life. Total yearly cost is the monthly fee times 12. Add equipment, activation, add-ons, and lost-device fees. Get the contract and cancellation terms in writing. Brands you'll often see include Medical Guardian and Bay Alarm Medical. Others are Lively and MobileHelp. Listing a brand isn't an endorsement. Once a device is set up, press the button together once. Then your parent knows what happens.

Do Medicare Advantage plans cover medical alert systems?

Some plans do. CMS lets Medicare Advantage plans offer in-home Personal Emergency Response devices as a supplemental benefit if they are primarily health related, and a PERS may not include cell phones (CMS Medicare Managed Care Manual, Ch. 4).

  1. Search the plan's Evidence of Coverage or Summary of Benefits. Those are the plan's yearly coverage documents. Look for "personal emergency response system," "PERS," or "medical alert."
  2. Or call member services using the number on your parent's plan card. Ask which device, what it costs, and who qualifies.
  3. Check again each year; benefits can change.
  4. Compare plans during Open Enrollment (October 15–December 7) with Medicare Plan Finder.

Where can people on fixed incomes get help paying for a medical alert system?

Start with Medicaid. Some states' home- and community-based services (HCBS) waivers cover a PERS, sometimes including installation and the monthly fee (Medicaid.gov fact sheets for Alabama and Michigan waivers). Medicaid is a joint federal and state program. It helps with health costs for people with limited income and resources. An HCBS waiver is a Medicaid program that pays for care at home or in the community instead of a nursing home (Medicaid.gov, 1915(c)).

  1. Call your parent's state Medicaid office or case manager. Ask whether their waiver covers a personal emergency response system. Medicaid.gov lists "personal emergency response systems-installation/monthly fee" in an Alabama waiver. It also lists a personal emergency response system in Michigan's MI Choice waiver.
  2. Expect eligibility rules. Waivers usually require Medicaid eligibility and a certain level of care need.
  3. Ask your local Area Agency on Aging about local help. Use the Eldercare Locator (eldercare.acl.gov or 1-800-677-1116).
  4. If your parent is a veteran enrolled in VA health care, ask their VA care team. VA is the federal agency for veterans' benefits. Ask whether a device or service is available. VA's Veteran-Directed Care gives eligible Veterans a budget. The budget is for home and community based services. Services vary by location.
  5. Long-term care insurance. Read the policy or call the insurer.

Is a medical alert system tax-deductible?

Possibly, and only if you itemize. IRS (the U.S. tax agency) Publication 502 (2025) doesn't name medical alert systems, so a cost can count only if it fits the IRS definition of a medical expense (IRS Topic 501; IRS Publication 502 (2025)). To itemize means you list deductions on Schedule A. You do this instead of taking the standard deduction.

  1. Check the definition. The IRS says medical expenses are "the costs of diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease and for the purpose of affecting any part or function of the body" (IRS Topic 501; IRS Publication 502 (2025)).
  2. Check the threshold. Medical expenses are deducted on Schedule A (Form 1040). Only the part above 7.5% of adjusted gross income counts. AGI is income after certain adjustments, shown on the tax return (2025 returns) (IRS Topic 501; IRS Publication 502 (2025)).
  3. If you pay for your parent, the IRS says you can include those medical expenses. They must be expenses you paid for your dependent. Publication 502 explains who qualifies (IRS Topic 501; IRS Publication 502 (2025)).
  4. Keep receipts and ask a tax professional before claiming it.

Can a smartwatch replace a medical alert system?

It depends on the watch and on your parent. Check the maker's guide for what the watch can do, and don't expect Medicare to pay for it. Original Medicare denies alert systems (CMS NCD 280.1). A Medicare Advantage PERS benefit can't include cellular telephones (CMS Medicare Managed Care Manual, Ch. 4).

  1. Check the watch's features in the maker's guide. Look for fall detection and emergency calling. Also check whether it needs its own cellular plan.
  2. Be honest about daily use. Will your parent charge it every day? Will they wear it in the shower and at night?
  3. Decide who should answer a call for help. Check the maker's guide for the watch. Check the provider's written terms for a medical alert service. See who actually receives the call, and when.
  4. Don't expect plan coverage for it. Original Medicare denies alert systems (CMS NCD 280.1). A Medicare Advantage PERS benefit can't include a cell phone (CMS Medicare Managed Care Manual, Ch. 4).
