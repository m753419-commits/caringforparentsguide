Can I Claim My Parent as a Dependent? Taxes for Family Caregivers (2026 Tax Year)

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 3, 2026

US programs only.

Please note: This post is general information, not legal, financial, or medical advice.

Tax rules change each year, so check your return with a tax professional or free IRS (the U.S. tax agency) help through VITA or TCE (IRS programs that offer free basic tax return preparation to qualified individuals) (IRS; full disclaimer).

Can I claim my parent as a dependent?

Possibly: Your parent can be your qualifying relative, a type of dependent (a person you can list on your tax return because you support them), if four IRS tests are met: not a qualifying child (a child who counts as someone’s dependent under separate rules), member of household or relationship, gross income, and support (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).

  1. Relationship: your father or mother doesn't have to live with you all year as a member of your household to meet this test (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  2. Gross income (all income received as money, goods, property, and services that isn't exempt from tax): for the 2026 tax year, your parent's gross income must be less than $5,300 (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32; a Rev. Proc., or revenue procedure, is official IRS guidance); for the 2025 tax year, the limit is $5,200 (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  3. Support: you must generally provide more than half of your parent's total support for the year, and your parent's own money counts as their own support only if it is actually spent for support; Worksheet 2 in Publication 501 can help (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  4. Citizenship and joint returns: your parent must be a U.S. citizen, U.S. resident alien (a non-citizen who meets IRS residency rules), U.S. national (a person who owes permanent allegiance to the U.S.), or a resident of Canada or Mexico, and generally can't file a joint return (one return filed together by a married couple) with a spouse, except one filed only to claim a refund of tax withheld or estimated tax paid (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  5. If your parent is in a nursing home: Publication 501 says that if a person is placed in a nursing home for an indefinite period of time to receive constant medical care, the absence may be considered temporary (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).

What if my siblings and I share the cost?

Use a multiple support agreement: If two or more of you together provide more than half of your parent's support, you can agree that one of you who individually provides more than 10% claims your parent (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).

  1. Everyone else signs. Each of the others must sign a statement agreeing not to claim your parent for that year (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  2. Attach the declaration. The person claiming your parent must attach a multiple support declaration to their return; Form 2120 can be used for this purpose (IRS Form 2120).
  3. Check with the IRS tool: the Interactive Tax Assistant answers "Whom may I claim as a dependent?" (IRS).
  4. Agree in writing as a family. See our guide to splitting care among siblings.

What tax breaks can I get for a dependent parent?

Two may apply: The credit for other dependents (a tax credit, an amount subtracted from the tax you owe) is up to $500 for each dependent (2025 tax year), and you may be able to file as head of household (a filing status, the category you file under, that can lower your tax) even if your parent doesn't live with you (IRS; IRS Publication 501 (2025)).

  1. Credit for other dependents: it is non-refundable (it can lower your tax but isn't paid to you as a refund); it begins to decrease if your adjusted gross income (AGI: your total income minus certain adjustments) exceeds $200,000 ($400,000 for married filing jointly, a married couple filing one return together) (2025 tax year), and the IRS has not published a 2026 change as of November 2026 (IRS; Schedule 8812 instructions (2025)).
  2. Your parent needs a TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number: an identification number used by the IRS, issued by the Social Security Administration (the federal agency that pays retirement and disability benefits) or the IRS), such as a Social Security number or ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number), to count for this credit (IRS; IRS).
  3. Head of household: if your qualifying person is your parent, you can file this way even if your parent doesn't live with you, but you must be able to claim your parent and pay more than half the cost of keeping up your parent's main home for the year; paying more than half the cost of a rest home or home for the elderly counts (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  4. 2026 standard deduction (a set dollar amount that reduces the income you're taxed on): $16,100 for single filers, $24,150 for heads of household, and $32,200 for married couples filing jointly (2026 tax year) (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32; IRS Topic 551).
  5. If your parent files their own return: the additional standard deduction for age 65 or older or blind is $1,650, or $2,050 if unmarried and not a surviving spouse (2026 tax year) (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32); and the IRS says that, effective for 2025 through 2028, individuals who are age 65 and older may claim an additional deduction of $6,000, which phases out for modified adjusted gross income (your income figure used for this tax rule, from your tax return) over $75,000 ($150,000 for joint filers) (IRS).

Can I deduct my parent's medical bills?

Possibly: You can include medical expenses you paid for your dependent, but you can deduct only the part of your total medical and dental expenses that is more than 7.5% of your AGI (IRS Publication 502 (2025); IRS).

  1. You must itemize (list deductions such as medical expenses on Schedule A instead of taking the standard deduction) (IRS Publication 502 (2025); IRS Topic 501).
  2. Even if your parent isn't your dependent only because their gross income was $5,200 or more, they filed a joint return, or you could be claimed by someone else, you can include medical expenses you paid for them (2025 tax year) (IRS Publication 502 (2025)).
  3. Nursing home and nursing care: meals and lodging count if a principal reason for being in the home is to get medical care; otherwise only the medical or nursing care part counts, and wages for nursing services can count even if the worker isn't a nurse (IRS Publication 502 (2025)).
  4. Premiums and mileage: Medicare (the federal health insurance program for people 65 and older) Part B (medical insurance) and Part D (drug coverage) premiums count (IRS Publication 502 (2025); Medicare.gov); long-term care insurance premiums count up to $500 (age 40 or less), $930 (41–50), $1,860 (51–60), $4,960 (61–70), or $6,200 (over 70) (2026 tax year) (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32); the medical mileage rate is 20.5 cents a mile for January 1–June 30, 2026, and 23.5 cents for July 1–December 31, 2026 (IRS).
  5. More detail: see our cost guide and medical alert systems and taxes.

Can I get a tax credit for my parent's care while I work?

Possibly: The child and dependent care credit can cover care for a parent who wasn't physically or mentally able to care for themselves and lived with you for more than half the year, if you pay for the care so you can work or look for work (IRS Publication 503 (2025)).

  1. Qualifying person (someone whose care counts for this credit): Publication 503 says persons who can't dress, clean, or feed themselves because of physical or mental disabilities, or who must have constant attention to prevent them from injuring themselves or others, are considered not able to care for themselves (IRS Publication 503 (2025)).
  2. Care outside your home counts if your parent regularly spends at least 8 hours each day in your home, and a dependent care center counts only if it complies with all state and local regulations that apply (IRS Publication 503 (2025)).
  3. Who you pay matters: you can't count amounts paid to someone you can claim as a dependent, to your child under age 19, or to your spouse (IRS Publication 503 (2025)).
  4. Limits: beginning with tax year 2026, the maximum credit rate increases from 35% to 50%, and qualifying expenses stay capped at $3,000 for one person or $6,000 for two or more; the rate is lower at higher incomes, so see the Form 2441 instructions (IRS; IRS Form 2441).

Should I use a dependent care FSA?

It can help: For the 2026 tax year, the annual dependent care FSA (flexible spending arrangement, an employer benefit for care costs) limit is $7,500 ($3,750 for married filing separately) (IRS Publication 15-B (2026)).

  1. What it is: a dependent care FSA is an employer benefit, and the IRS lets dependent care assistance be excluded from wages (IRS Publication 15-B (2026)).
  2. No double counting: if you get dependent care benefits, you must subtract them from the dollar limit you use for the credit (IRS Topic 602; IRS Publication 503 (2025)).
  3. Report it on Form 2441 and ask your employer about enrollment (IRS).

What if I hire a caregiver or get paid to care for my parent?

Check your tax duties: If you pay cash wages of $3,000 or more in 2026 to any one household employee (someone you pay to work in your home, such as a caregiver), you withhold and pay social security and Medicare taxes (IRS Publication 926 (2026)).

  1. Household employee: the IRS says if you have one in 2026, you may need to pay state and federal employment taxes; the taxes are 15.3% of cash wages, and your employee's share is 7.65% (IRS Publication 926 (2026)).
  2. If you're paid through Medicaid (a joint federal and state program that helps with health costs for people with limited income and resources): Notice 2014-7 says the IRS will treat certain Medicaid waiver payments (Medicaid programs that pay for long-term care services at home or in the community instead of in an institution) as difficulty of care payments (payments for the extra care a person needs because of a condition) excludable from gross income; see our guide to getting paid (IRS).
  3. Free tax help: the TCE program offers free tax help particularly for those 60 and older; use the VITA Locator Tool or call 800-906-9887 (IRS).
  4. Check a preparer: the IRS Directory of Federal Tax Return Preparers with Credentials and Select Qualifications lists preparers in your area (IRS).
  5. Deadlines: the IRS has not published the 2027 filing deadline or 2027 amounts as of November 2026; if the 15th falls on a weekend or legal holiday, the due date moves to the next business day (IRS).
