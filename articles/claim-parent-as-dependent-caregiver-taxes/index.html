Can I Claim My Parent as a Dependent? Taxes for Family Caregivers (2026 Tax Year)

Family Caregiver Practical Guide · Updated November 7, 2026

US programs only.

Please note: This post is general information, not legal, financial, or medical advice.

Tax rules change each year. Check your return with a tax professional or free IRS (the U.S. tax agency) help. Free help comes through VITA or TCE (IRS programs that offer free basic tax return preparation to qualified individuals) (IRS; full disclaimer).

In plain words: This guide explains taxes when you help pay for a parent. You may be able to list your parent on your tax return. Some care costs may also lower your tax. First, check the tests that decide if your parent counts.

Can I claim my parent as a dependent?

Possibly, if your parent passes four IRS tests for a qualifying relative. That is a type of dependent (a person you can list on your tax return because you support them) (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).

  1. The four tests. The first is not a qualifying child (a child who counts as someone’s dependent under separate rules). The others are member of household or relationship, gross income, and support. Your father or mother can meet the relationship test. They don't have to live with you all year as a member of your household (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  2. Gross income has a limit. It means all income received as money, goods, property, and services that isn't exempt from tax. For the 2026 tax year, your parent's gross income must be less than $5,300 (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32; a Rev. Proc., or revenue procedure, is official IRS guidance). For the 2025 tax year, the limit is $5,200 (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  3. Support. You must generally provide more than half of your parent's total support for the year. Your parent's own money counts as their own support. But it counts only if it is actually spent for support. Worksheet 2 in Publication 501 can help (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  4. Citizenship and joint returns. Your parent must be a U.S. citizen or a resident of Canada or Mexico. They can also be a U.S. resident alien (a non-citizen who meets IRS residency rules). Or they can be a U.S. national (a person who owes permanent allegiance to the U.S.). Your parent generally can't file a joint return with a spouse. That is one return filed together by a married couple. There is one exception: a return filed only to claim a refund of tax withheld or estimated tax paid (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  5. Nursing home stays. Publication 501 says that if a person is placed in a nursing home for an indefinite period of time to receive constant medical care, the absence may be considered temporary (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).

What if my siblings and I share the cost?

Use a multiple support agreement if together you provide more than half of your parent's support. You can agree that one of you who individually provides more than 10% claims your parent (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).

  1. Everyone else signs. Each of the others must sign a statement. It says they agree not to claim your parent for that year (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  2. Attach the declaration. The person claiming your parent must attach a multiple support declaration to their return. Form 2120 can be used for this purpose (IRS Form 2120).
  3. Check with the IRS tool. The Interactive Tax Assistant answers "Whom may I claim as a dependent?" (IRS).
  4. Agree in writing as a family. See our guide to splitting care among siblings.

What tax breaks can I get for a dependent parent?

The credit for other dependents (a tax credit, an amount subtracted from the tax you owe) may apply. It is up to $500 for each dependent (2025 tax year) (IRS; IRS Publication 501 (2025)).

  1. Credit for other dependents. It is non-refundable. That means it can lower your tax but isn't paid to you as a refund. It begins to decrease above a set income. That point is when your adjusted gross income (AGI: your total income minus certain adjustments) exceeds $200,000. For married filing jointly (a married couple filing one return together), that point is when it exceeds $400,000 (2025 tax year). The IRS has not published a 2026 change as of November 2026 (IRS; Schedule 8812 instructions (2025)).
  2. Your parent needs a TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number) to count for this credit. A TIN is an identification number used by the IRS. It is issued by the Social Security Administration (the federal agency that pays retirement and disability benefits) or the IRS. Examples are a Social Security number or ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) (IRS; IRS).
  3. Head of household (a filing status, the category you file under, that can lower your tax) can help. If your qualifying person is your parent, you can file this way. Your parent doesn't have to live with you. But you must be able to claim your parent. You must also pay more than half the cost of keeping up your parent's main home for the year. Paying more than half the cost of a rest home or home for the elderly counts too (IRS Publication 501 (2025)).
  4. 2026 standard deduction. This is a set dollar amount that reduces the income you're taxed on. It is $16,100 for single filers and $24,150 for heads of household. It is $32,200 for married couples filing jointly (2026 tax year) (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32; IRS Topic 551).
  5. Your parent's own return. There is an additional standard deduction for age 65 or older or blind. It is $1,650. It is $2,050 if unmarried and not a surviving spouse (2026 tax year) (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). The IRS also has a rule for 2025 through 2028. It says individuals age 65 and older may claim an additional deduction of $6,000. Its phase-out uses modified adjusted gross income (your income figure used for this tax rule, from your tax return). The phase-out starts over $75,000 ($150,000 for joint filers) (IRS).

Can I deduct my parent's medical bills?

Possibly: You can include medical expenses you paid for your dependent. But you can deduct only the part of your total medical and dental expenses that is more than 7.5% of your AGI (IRS Publication 502 (2025); IRS).

  1. You must itemize (list deductions such as medical expenses on Schedule A instead of taking the standard deduction) (IRS Publication 502 (2025); IRS Topic 501).
  2. Even if your parent isn't your dependent, you can include medical expenses you paid for them. This applies when the only reason they aren't your dependent is one or more of these. Their gross income was $5,200 or more (2025 tax year). They filed a joint return. Or you could be claimed by someone else (IRS Publication 502 (2025)).
  3. Nursing homes. Meals and lodging can count. They count if a principal reason for being in the home is to get medical care. Otherwise, only the medical or nursing care part counts. Wages for nursing services can count even if the worker isn't a nurse (IRS Publication 502 (2025)).
  4. Premiums and mileage. Medicare is the federal health insurance program for people 65 and older and some younger people with disabilities. Its Part B (medical insurance) and Part D (drug coverage) premiums count (IRS Publication 502 (2025); Medicare.gov). Long-term care insurance premiums count up to a limit by age. The limit is $500 (age 40 or less) or $930 (41–50). It is $1,860 (51–60), $4,960 (61–70), or $6,200 (over 70) (2026 tax year) (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). The medical mileage rate is 20.5 cents a mile for January 1–June 30, 2026. It is 23.5 cents for July 1–December 31, 2026 (IRS).
  5. More detail. See our cost guide and medical alert systems and taxes.

Can I get a tax credit for my parent's care while I work?

Possibly, if you pay for your parent's care so you can work or look for work. The child and dependent care credit can cover a parent who wasn't physically or mentally able to care for themselves and lived with you for more than half the year (IRS Publication 503 (2025)).

  1. Qualifying person. This is someone whose care counts for this credit. Your parent must have lived with you for more than half the year. Publication 503 says a person is considered not able to care for themselves if they can't dress, clean, or feed themselves because of physical or mental disabilities. The same is true of a person who must have constant attention to prevent them from injuring themselves or others (IRS Publication 503 (2025)).
  2. Care outside your home can count. Your parent must regularly spend at least 8 hours each day in your home. A dependent care center counts too. But it must comply with all state and local regulations that apply (IRS Publication 503 (2025)).
  3. Who you pay matters. You can't count amounts paid to someone you can claim as a dependent. You also can't count amounts paid to your spouse. The same goes for your child under age 19 (IRS Publication 503 (2025)).
  4. Limits. Beginning with tax year 2026, the maximum credit rate increases from 35% to 50%. Qualifying expenses stay capped at $3,000 for one person. The cap is $6,000 for two or more. The rate is lower at higher incomes, so see the Form 2441 instructions (IRS; IRS Form 2441).

Should I use a dependent care FSA?

A dependent care FSA (flexible spending arrangement, an employer benefit for care costs) can help. For the 2026 tax year, its annual limit is $7,500 ($3,750 for married filing separately) (IRS Publication 15-B (2026)).

  1. What it is. A dependent care FSA is an employer benefit. The IRS lets dependent care assistance be excluded from wages (left out of your wages) (IRS Publication 15-B (2026)).
  2. No double counting. You may get dependent care benefits. If so, you must subtract them from the dollar limit you use for the credit (IRS Topic 602; IRS Publication 503 (2025)).
  3. Report it on Form 2441 and ask your employer about enrollment (IRS).

What if I hire a caregiver or get paid to care for my parent?

If you pay cash wages of $3,000 or more in 2026 to any one household employee, you withhold and pay social security and Medicare taxes. A household employee is someone you pay to work in your home, such as a caregiver (IRS Publication 926 (2026)).

  1. Household employee in 2026? The IRS says you may need to pay state and federal employment taxes. The taxes are 15.3% of cash wages. Your employee's share is 7.65% (IRS Publication 926 (2026)).
  2. Paid through Medicaid? Medicaid is a joint federal and state program. It helps with health costs for people with limited income and resources. Medicaid waiver programs are Medicaid programs that pay for long-term care services at home or in the community. The care happens there instead of in an institution. Their payments are called Medicaid waiver payments. Notice 2014-7 says the IRS will treat certain Medicaid waiver payments as difficulty of care payments. These are excludable from gross income (not counted as income). Difficulty of care payments are payments for the extra care a person needs because of a condition. See our guide to getting paid (IRS).
  3. Free tax help. The TCE program offers free tax help, particularly for those 60 and older. Use the VITA Locator Tool or call 800-906-9887 (IRS).
  4. Check a preparer. Use the IRS Directory of Federal Tax Return Preparers with Credentials and Select Qualifications. It lists preparers in your area (IRS).
  5. Deadlines. The IRS has not published the 2027 filing deadline or 2027 amounts as of November 2026. The 15th may fall on a weekend or legal holiday. Then the due date moves to the next business day (IRS).
