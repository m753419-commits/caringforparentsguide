Moving an Aging Parent Into Your Home: Questions to Ask First

By Ellen Carter. Published October 31, 2026. Publisher: Family Caregiver Practical Guide.

When my mother's house became too much for her, my first instinct was simple: she'll move in with us. We had a spare room. I loved her. How hard could it be?

My husband, wisely, asked a few questions I hadn't. Who would be home during the day? What would happen to our Friday nights? Was the spare room on the ground floor? How would Mom feel being a guest in someone else's kitchen after fifty years of running her own?

We did eventually make it work, but only after weeks of honest conversations, a few changes to the house, and some written-down agreements that saved us more than one argument. This guide covers the questions to ask before you say yes, and how to set up the move so it works for everyone.

Please note: This post is general information, not medical, legal, or financial advice. Moving a parent in can affect taxes, benefits, and legal arrangements. Talk with a qualified financial professional or elder law attorney in your state before making major money or property decisions.

Key takeaways

  • Ask honest questions about care needs, your household, and money before deciding.
  • Involve your parent and everyone who lives in your home.
  • Prepare the house for safety before moving day.
  • Write down agreements about money, chores, and privacy.
  • Plan for breaks and for what happens if needs change.

Is Moving In the Right Choice?

Living together can be wonderful: more time together, less worry, sometimes lower costs. It can also strain marriages, careers, and the parent-child relationship. It's one option among several. Our comparison of in-home care vs. assisted living covers alternatives worth weighing.

Questions to Ask Before You Decide

About Your Parent

  • ☐ What does your parent want? Have you asked them directly?
  • ☐ How much help do they need now, and how might that change?
  • ☐ Will they be alone during the day? Is that safe?
  • ☐ Will they lose friends, activities, or their community by moving?
  • ☐ How will they feel about house rules that aren't theirs?

About Your Household

  • ☐ Does everyone living in your home agree, including your partner and children?
  • ☐ Who will provide day-to-day help, and for how many hours?
  • ☐ How will this affect your work? (Our guide to caring for a parent while working covers leave and flexibility options.)
  • ☐ Is there a private space for your parent, ideally on the ground floor with a bathroom nearby?
  • ☐ What happens to your own routines, privacy, and time together?

About Money

  • ☐ Will your parent contribute to household costs? How much?
  • ☐ Who pays for changes to the house?
  • ☐ What happens to your parent's home: is it sold, rented, or kept?
  • ☐ Have you talked with a financial professional about taxes or benefits?

About Your Family

  • ☐ How will your siblings contribute, even if they don't live nearby? See how to split caregiving responsibilities among siblings.
  • ☐ Will there be resentment about money, inheritance, or who does the work?

Have the Conversation

Bring your parent and household together for a calm, unhurried talk. Our guide on how to talk to your aging parent about needing help has openers that work here too, such as:

  • "We'd love to have you closer. What would make living here feel like home to you?"
  • "What worries you most about moving?"
  • "What would you want to keep doing on your own?"

Consider a trial: a longer visit of a few weeks can show everyone what living together is really like.

Get the House Ready

Before moving day, walk through your home with our home safety checklist. Common priorities:

  • ☐ A bedroom on the main floor, if possible
  • ☐ Grab bars in the bathroom, properly anchored
  • ☐ Night-lights from the bedroom to the bathroom
  • ☐ Clear walkways, with throw rugs removed or secured
  • ☐ Handrails on stairs
  • ☐ A comfortable chair with armrests in shared spaces
  • ☐ A place for your parent's own furniture, photos, and favorite things

Bringing familiar belongings helps a new room feel like their own.

Write Down Your Agreements

It may feel awkward, but a simple written agreement prevents a lot of misunderstanding. Cover:

Topic Questions to settle
Money Will your parent contribute to rent, food, or utilities? How much and how often?
Chores What will your parent do? What will others do?
Meals Eat together every night, or separately sometimes?
Privacy When is the door closed? Who can enter whose room?
Guests Can your parent have visitors? Can you still host friends?
Breaks When do you get time off, and who covers?
Review When will you check in on how it's going?

If money changes hands, or if your parent is giving or selling property to family, talk with an elder law attorney or financial professional. Some families put a written personal care agreement in place when a family member is paid for care; a professional can explain whether that makes sense for you.

Protect Everyone's Wellbeing

  • Keep your parent connected. Help them find a senior center, faith community, or club nearby. The Eldercare Locator (eldercare.acl.gov, 1-800-677-1116) can help you find local programs.
  • Protect your relationship. Keep time for your partner and children.
  • Schedule breaks. Regular respite is essential. Our respite care guide explains the options.
  • Ask for help. Use our scripts on how to ask for help as a caregiver.
  • Watch for burnout. Review the caregiver burnout warning signs every few months.

Plan for Change

Needs change over time. Agree now on what you'll do if your parent needs more care than you can provide: more in-home help, adult day programs, or a move to assisted living. Talking about it in advance makes a future decision less painful. Our guide to what it costs to care for an aging parent can help you plan for those costs, and our documents checklist helps you make sure the paperwork is in place.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should my elderly parent move in with me?

It depends on your parent's wishes and care needs, your household, your work, and your finances. Ask honest questions with everyone involved, and consider a trial stay before deciding.

How do I prepare my home for an elderly parent?

Start with safety: a main-floor bedroom if possible, bathroom grab bars, good lighting, handrails, and clear walkways. Make room for your parent's own belongings so the space feels like home.

Should my parent pay rent if they live with me?

Many families agree on some contribution to household costs, but there's no single right answer. Write down what you agree, and talk with a financial professional about any tax or benefit effects.

How do I keep my marriage healthy when my parent lives with us?

Agree as a couple before the move, protect regular time together, set clear house rules, and schedule breaks. Check in with each other often about how it's going.

What if living together stops working?

Plan for that possibility from the start. Options may include more in-home help, adult day programs, or assisted living. Agreeing in advance on when to revisit the arrangement makes the conversation easier.
