About

Family Caregiver Practical Guide is a small set of practical articles for adults in the United States who are helping an aging parent.

The guides cover the ordinary problems that show up between appointments. They are written in plain language. Each one has a table or checklist a family can copy, and each one links to public sources. The topics are:

  • Medicare: reviewing a plan during Open Enrollment, late-enrollment penalties, lowering IRMAA (an extra Medicare charge for higher incomes) with Form SSA-44, Medigap Plan G vs. Plan N, a missed Medigap sign-up period, reading a Medicare Summary Notice, Medicare Advantage rehab denials and appeals, hospital discharge and home health, and hospice.
  • Paying for Care: what care costs, Medicaid and the nursing home look-back, getting paid as a family caregiver, and claiming a parent as a dependent on your taxes.
  • Paperwork and legal steps: a documents checklist, and guardianship vs. power of attorney.
  • Safety, health, and where a parent lives: home safety, early signs of dementia, signs a parent can no longer live alone, in-home care vs. assisted living, comparing and visiting nursing homes, moving a parent into your home, talking about driving, medical alert systems, and GPS trackers.
  • Caring for the caregiver: caregiver burnout, respite care, free support groups and resources, working while caregiving and FMLA, splitting care among siblings, and long-distance caregiving.

Who it is for

The site is written for adult children. It is also useful for anyone coordinating care for an older parent. Examples talk about mothers and fathers. Many of the same questions apply to a parent-in-law or another older relative. Programs and legal authority still depend on that person's situation and on the state where they live.

The guides use a United States frame of reference: Medicare, Medicaid, Social Security, veterans' benefits, and state law. If your parent lives in another country, a checklist may still help you list what to gather, but the programs named here will not match.

Who publishes it

The publisher is Family Caregiver Practical Guide. It is not a clinic, a law firm, a financial advisory firm, an insurance agency, or a government agency. All guides are published under the site's name, Family Caregiver Practical Guide, not under a personal byline. The guides are based on public sources, which each guide links to. They are not based on anyone's personal caregiving story.

Editorial and fact-checking

Family Caregiver Practical Guide writes, checks, and updates every guide. No doctor, lawyer, or licensed financial or insurance professional reviews them, so please confirm decisions with one.

Each guide is based on official sources where they exist, such as Medicare.gov, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), the Social Security Administration (SSA), and other government agencies. Other public sources are named where a guide uses them, and every guide lists its sources at the end. Figures, deadlines, form names, and phone numbers are checked against those sources before a guide goes up.

Each guide shows the date it was last updated or checked, near the top. When an official figure changes, such as Medicare's yearly premiums and deductibles, the affected guides are updated and re-dated.

If you spot an error or something out of date, send a note through the contact page. Include the page and, if you can, the official source. Confirmed errors are fixed.

How the guides are meant to be used

Copy the checklist. Talk with your parent while they can still say what they want. Then confirm medical, legal, and financial steps with a qualified professional. The disclaimer is the full statement of those limits.

What this site does

It publishes general information and checklists. Diagnosis, facility placement, benefit calculations, tax results, and legal documents belong with a qualified professional who can look at your parent's situation.

This site does not take payment from readers and does not ask for bank details, passwords, insurance numbers, or medical records. Some guides mention a workbook by name. This website does not sell that book and does not process orders. Some guides include Amazon affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate, Family Caregiver Practical Guide earns from qualifying purchases. You do not pay more. See the disclaimer for details.

Public services named in the guides, including the Eldercare Locator, Medicare, and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, are independent of this site.
